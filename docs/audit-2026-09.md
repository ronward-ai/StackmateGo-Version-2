# StackMate Go — full audit, September 2026

A sweep across data integrity, security, pre-launch readiness, dead code and test coverage.

**Baseline at the time of writing:** `tsc` clean · 332 unit tests pass (20 files) · 44 Firestore
rules tests pass against the emulator. Nothing listed here is currently *failing*. It is all latent —
which is why it needs writing down rather than fixing in a panic.

**Status — updated as findings are cleared.** Fixed so far: **C1, H1, H2, H3, H4, H5, H6, M1, M2, M3,
M4, M6, M7, M8, M9, M10, M11**, the whole **Delete** section bar two items noted below, most of **Correctness
debt** (currency, ordinals, the payout formula, the participant payout read, the event-name read), and
all of **Pre-launch polish** plus the wake lock. Each has a commit that names its finding.

**H2 shipped as a combination of two of the three shapes the finding laid out**: `leagueSettings` took
the real fix (deterministic per-league document id, `lib/leagueSettingsId.ts`, so `list` could become
owner-only without breaking the participant read) rather than the compromise — it uniquely leaked an
account identifier, which the other three did not. `seasons`/`leaguePlayers`/`tournamentResults` took
the "require any session" compromise, since they are genuinely one-to-many per league and cannot
collapse to a single `get`; closing that required gating four `list` queries on `isAuthenticated` and
fixing a real race in the process (the participant view's anonymous sign-in is fire-and-forget, and
nothing previously waited for it — exactly the situation `read: if true` was chosen to sidestep the
first time, per CLAUDE.md).

**M6, M7 and M8 shipped together**, being the same subsystem (`server/routes.ts`,
`client/src/hooks/useSubscription.ts`) and needing the same decisions to proceed: confirmed Railway is
production and Firebase Hosting's deploy is a stale leftover, and a downgrade policy (a failed payment
loses Pro immediately; a cancellation keeps Pro until the paid period actually ends). `useSubscription`
now asks the server at runtime (`GET /api/payments-status`) instead of inferring from
`VITE_API_BASE_URL`, a variable that was never about payments; the checkout endpoint verifies a
Firebase ID token instead of trusting `uid`/`email` from the body, and rate-limits by uid; the webhook
handles `customer.subscription.updated` and orders events by `event.created` rather than trusting
delivery order. `server/lib/subscriptionStatus.ts` and `server/lib/rateLimit.ts` carry the pure,
tested decisions; `tests/server/routes.test.ts` covers the wiring with Stripe and firebase-admin
mocked, mutation-checked the same way the rest of this audit's fixes were.

**M11 was reported from the field rather than found by this sweep** — device-local storage crossing
between accounts on one browser, which the audit missed because it asked who could read each key and
never what happens when the signed-in user changes. Fixed with per-account storage buckets and by
moving the director's setup into `userSettings/{uid}`, so it now follows the account between devices
instead of living on one.

**M10 is closed.** Its suggested fix could not be applied as written — `reportSyncFailure` was a
closure inside `PokerTimer`, so the hooks that needed it had no way to reach it. `lib/syncReporter.ts`
holds the streak at module scope instead, one for the whole app, and the sites the finding named
route through it. Two things turned out worse than the write-up: the rebuy path released its claim
BEFORE the await, so a failed removal was never retried and left a wrong finishing position
permanently; and a failed season create handed back a season that does not exist, which one of its
two callers then treated as success. The storage half got a health signal of its own — a standing
banner rather than a toast, since it is a condition — and that landed in one place rather than the
eight the finding had to list, because those writes now funnel through `lib/scopedStorage.ts`.

**Still open, and why:** nothing. `LeagueStatsTracked` and the dead `displaySettings` flags
are deliberately left: `statsToTrack` gates a shape check on persisted settings, so removing it could
silently reset a director's scoring, and twelve dead booleans are not worth that.

Findings are ranked by severity and each carries a file:line, what actually goes wrong, and a
suggested fix. Where a finding was surprising it was reproduced rather than reasoned about; those are
marked **Reproduced**.

The last section, [What is clean](#what-is-clean), records what the sweep cleared. That is half the
value of an audit — it stops the same ground being re-covered.

## Critical

### C1 — The Stripe webhook cannot receive a single event

**`server/index.ts:10`** + **`server/routes.ts:119-127`** · **Reproduced**

`express.json()` is registered at app level on line 10. `registerRoutes` runs at line 69, so the
webhook's hand-rolled raw-body collector is registered *after* it. The comment directly above that
collector states the requirement and the code does the opposite:

```js
// server/routes.ts:118
// Raw body required for signature verification — must come before express.json
```

`express.json()` consumes the request stream to EOF. The collector then attaches `data`/`end`
listeners to an already-ended stream. Node does not re-emit `end`, so `next()` is never called and
**the request hangs until Stripe times out.**

Reproduced by rebuilding the exact middleware order in a scratch Express app and POSTing a
Stripe-shaped JSON body: no response, request hung until aborted at 3 seconds.

**What it costs:** `customer.subscription.created` and `invoice.paid` never reach `setStatus('pro')`.
A customer pays and is never upgraded. Stripe retries for days and every retry fails identically. The
careful fail-loud 500s at `routes.ts:152-157` and `:183-188` — which are correct, and exist precisely
so an upgrade is never silently dropped — are never reached.

Payments are currently switched off, so this has cost nothing yet. It would have failed on day one.

**Fix** (verified working in the same scratch harness — the webhook receives the raw Buffer while
other routes still get parsed JSON):

```js
// server/index.ts, BEFORE the express.json() line
app.use('/api/stripe-webhook', express.raw({ type: 'application/json', limit: '1mb' }));
app.use(express.json({ limit: '1mb' }));
```

Then pass `req.body` (a Buffer) straight to `constructEvent` and delete the collector at
`routes.ts:119-127`.

Signature verification itself (`routes.ts:137-141`) is otherwise correct.

## High

Exploitable, or actively wrong today.

### H1 — "Unlimited rebuys" is capped at three, and `0` means three different things

`maxRebuys` is read at three sites, each spelling the fallback `|| 3`:

- **`client/src/hooks/useTournament.ts:1319`** — the actual gate
- **`client/src/components/TablesSection.tsx:583`**
- **`client/src/components/PlayerSection.tsx:896`**

`0` is how the Buy-in tab stores "unlimited": the input placeholder is `∞` and
**`BuyInSection.tsx:407`** renders `Max: {maxRebuys || 'unlimited'}`. But `0 || 3` is `3`.

**A director who sets rebuys to unlimited gets three.**

Re-entries have the mirror-image bug with a *different* operator:

- **`TablesSection.tsx:618`** — `maxReEntries ?? 99`, so `0` correctly survives and means *none
  allowed*
- **`TournamentInfoCard.tsx:604`** and **`ParticipantTournamentInfoCard.tsx:220`** — print a cap only
  when `> 0`, so `0` reads as *unlimited*

One fact, four answers: `0` means "three", "none" and "unlimited" depending on which file you are in.
This is the class CLAUDE.md documents twice over (the rake formula, the nine prize-pool copies), and
it is the likeliest finding here to have already affected a real game.

**Fix:** `client/src/lib/entryLimits.ts` owning `rebuysAllowed(structure, player)` and
`reEntriesAllowed(...)`, with `0` meaning unlimited everywhere, colocated tests, and all four call
sites routed through it. Take M1 in the same change.

### H2 — Four collections are world-listable: every player's name, unauthenticated

**`firestore.rules:110, 118, 126, 198`**

`allow read: if true` covers **`list`** as well as `get`. The deliberate get/list split applied to
`leagues:75-76` and `activeTournaments:156-157` was never applied to `seasons`, `leaguePlayers`,
`tournamentResults` or `leagueSettings` — even though the comments around them show the distinction
was understood.

With only the `projectId` and `databaseId` from the public bundle (`client/src/lib/firebase.ts:20-21`,
with `KNOWN_DATABASE_ID` hardcoded at `:18` as a fallback), one unauthenticated REST call paginates
out:

| Collection | What leaks |
|---|---|
| `leaguePlayers` | **Every player's real name**, in every league, for every user of the app |
| `tournamentResults` | Every finishing position, prize, buy-in, rebuy count and date |
| `seasons` | Every league's season structure |
| `leagueSettings` | Every director's Firebase uid, their leagueIds and points config |

These are named individuals at private home games.

**This is not a one-line fix, and the document should not pretend otherwise.** Firestore rules cannot
inspect a query's `where` clauses, and participants genuinely need filtered standings reads — the
justification at `firestore.rules:189-195` is sound as far as it goes. The realistic shapes:

1. Drop `leagueSettings` `list` to owner-only and key settings documents by `leagueId`, so
   participants reach them by `get` rather than by query. `useLeagueSettings.ts:299` already filters
   `where('userId','==',ownerId)`, so this is a document-id change, not a logic change.
2. Decide whether standings reads should require at least an anonymous session. The participant view
   already signs everyone in anonymously (`TournamentParticipantView.tsx:96`), so
   `allow list: if isAuthenticated()` would cost the app nothing and removes the
   no-account-required bulk dump. It does not stop a determined person, but it changes the shape of
   the attack from "one curl" to "sign in, then scrape".
3. Accept it for `tournamentResults`/`seasons` and close the other two.

Worth designing deliberately rather than guessing.

### H3 — Any signed-in visitor can eliminate or rename players in any live game

**`firestore.rules:181-184`**, playing out at **`client/src/hooks/useTournament.ts:441-466`**

The check-in branch requires only `isAuthenticated()` — which includes anonymous, and the participant
view signs every QR visitor in that way — plus `hasOnly(['players'])` and a preserved array length.
**The contents of each entry are unconstrained.**

CLAUDE.md concedes "a determined participant can still edit a field inside an existing entry". Tracing
what the director's client does with those fields shows the blast radius is larger than that:

- **`useTournament.ts:450-453`** — an incoming `isActive: false` is honoured as an **elimination**.
  Writing it busts a live player out on the director's screen and every other participant's phone.
- **`useTournament.ts:457-465`** — for active players the incoming entry is spread wholesale, so
  `name`, `chips`, `position` and `tableAssignment` all propagate. Every player on the big screen can
  be renamed to anything.
- `position` feeds `tournamentResults` when the game is recorded, so a mid-game tamper becomes a
  **permanent wrong league standing**.

`knockouts`/`rebuys`/`reEntries` are protected by the `Math.max` merge at `:460-462`, which is why the
damage lands on elimination and identity rather than counts.

A guest at the table with the QR code and a browser console can bust someone out.

**Fix, and it does not need the service-account key that org policy blocks:** move claims out of the
players array into a separate map field keyed by player id — `claims: { p1: deviceId }` — and narrow
the rule to `hasOnly(['claims'])`. The players array becomes director-only and this entire class
closes. `PlayerClaimView` already writes via REST with `?updateMask.fieldPaths=players`
(`PlayerClaimView.tsx:172`), so the payload change is small. This also removes M9 outright.

### H4 — `new Function` on a stored formula, executed in participants' browsers

**`client/src/hooks/useLeagueSettings.ts:170-176`**

```ts
const safeEval = new Function('f','p','k','b','c','z', /* … */ 'Math',
  `"use strict"; return (${formula.customFormula})`);
```

**There is no validation on this path.** The `allowedPattern` character-class check exists only in the
settings dialog's live preview (`LeagueSettingsDialog.tsx:826-838`) — a rendering concern that never
gates what is stored, and which `useLeagueSettings` never consults. `"use strict"` prevents nothing;
`constructor.constructor` and IIFEs are both reachable.

The trust boundary is what matters. **`RealTimeLeagueTable.tsx:61`** calls
`useLeagueSettings(tournament?.ownerId, …)` — the participant view loads the **director's** settings
and evaluates the director's formula in the **participant's** browser. That is by design, and
`firestore.rules:189-195` documents exactly why.

So any account holder can put arbitrary JavaScript in a points formula and have it execute on the
`stackmatego.com` origin in the browser of everyone who scans their QR code — with access to the
visitor's Firebase ID token, the ability to render a convincing sign-in overlay, and same-origin
access to everything the app can reach.

A *stranger* cannot poison someone else's settings: `create` requires `isDocUser()`
(`firestore.rules:199`) and the participant query filters by `userId`. So this is
director → participant, not attacker → anyone. It remains a stored-XSS delivery mechanism available
to any account holder.

**Fix:** apply the allow-list **at the evaluation site and at write time**, not in a preview. Better,
and in keeping with the rest of `lib/`: a small shunting-yard expression evaluator over an explicit
operator and variable set — roughly 80 lines, pure, testable, and exactly the shape
`lib/pointsBands.ts` already demonstrates. A CSP without `unsafe-eval` would kill `new Function`
outright, which is a good reason to do the parser first.

### H5 — The event logo can break saving entirely

**`client/src/components/SettingsSection.tsx:227`** → **`client/src/pages/PokerTimer.tsx:801`**

The logo upload reads the file with `readAsDataURL` — **no size limit, no downscaling** — into
`settings.branding.logoUrl`. `PokerTimer.tsx:801` syncs the whole `settings` object into the
tournament document.

A 4 MB photo from a phone camera roll becomes roughly 5.4 MB of base64. **Firestore's hard document
limit is 1 MiB**, so the write is rejected — and it is the *whole tournament document* write that
fails, not just the logo. The same string is also written to localStorage (~5 MB quota) and
re-downloaded by every participant on every snapshot.

The director sees a game that will not save, with no indication that the logo is the reason.

**Fix:** downscale through a canvas to a bounded dimension (512px is ample for a logo) and re-encode
as JPEG or WebP before storing; refuse anything still oversized with a message that names the cause.

This is the finding most likely to produce a mystifying "sync issue" on a real night.

### H6 — The winner's points are hard-coded to `players × 36`

**`client/src/hooks/useTournament.ts:1182`**

```js
points: totalPlayers * 36, // 1st place points
```

`36 × p` is literally the first-place value of *one* preset (`lib/pointsPresets.ts:35`,
`field-bands`). Every other scheme — "Close together", "Balanced", "Even steps", banded, or any custom
formula — disagrees. It is a **third** points evaluator alongside `calculatePoints` and the dialog's
validator.

Mitigating: the persisted standings come from `recordResultByName` → `calculatePointsFromSettings`, so
the league table itself is correct. But this value is written into state and broadcast to every
participant device via `broadcastTournamentAction('tournament_complete', finalState)`, so the number
shown at the moment of victory is wrong for every league not using that one preset.

**Fix:** call `calculatePoints` with the same six arguments the recording path uses.

## Medium

### M1 — The rebuy period is advertised and never enforced

**`client/src/components/BuyInSection.tsx:407`** promises *"Available during first {n} levels"*. The
gate at **`useTournament.ts:1319-1322`** checks only `allowRebuys` and the cap — it never looks at the
current level. `reEntryPeriodLevels` is the same story (`BuyInSection.tsx:471`).

Their sibling `addonAvailableLevel` **is** enforced (`PlayerSection.tsx:1006`), which is what makes
this an omission rather than a deliberate choice.

**Fix:** enforce both in the `entryLimits` module from H1, or remove the two controls. Enforcing is
the smaller surprise — the UI already promises it.

### M2 — `leagueId` is unpinned on update: inject documents into someone else's league

**`firestore.rules:113-114, 121-122, 129-130`**

```
allow update: if ownsLeague(resource.data.leagueId);
```

This checks the league the document **currently** belongs to. `request.resource.data.leagueId` is
unconstrained, so:

1. A registered user creates `tournamentResults/x` with their own `leagueId` — correctly allowed.
2. They update it, setting `leagueId` to a victim's league and `points` to anything. The rule reads
   the *stored* leagueId, still their own, and passes.

The document now appears in the victim's standings. The same works for `leaguePlayers` (inject a
phantom or abusive name into a stranger's table) and `seasons`. Victim leagueIds are trivially
obtained via H2.

The `create` path was carefully closed against exactly this — `tests/rules/firestore.rules.test.ts:254`
is titled *"stops a registered stranger creating documents in a league they do not own"* — and
`update` reopens it. No test covers moving one's *own* document into another league.

**Fix:** add `&& request.resource.data.leagueId == resource.data.leagueId` to all three `update`
rules, plus a regression test asserting
`updateDoc(doc(stranger(),'tournamentResults','own-result'), { leagueId: LEAGUE })` fails.

### M3 — `ownerId` is writable on `activeTournaments`, and the comment says it isn't

**`firestore.rules:159-161`** vs the comment at **`:145`**

The comment states *"ownerId is unwritable by clients"*. The owner branch validates only the **stored**
`ownerId` via `isExistingDocOwner()` and never constrains the incoming one.
`completedTournaments:211` and `tournamentTemplates:219` get this right
(`isExistingDocOwner() && isDocOwner()`); `activeTournaments` is missing the second half.

Consequences:

- A director can hand a live game to another account from the console — reintroducing the
  transfer-code handover that was deliberately removed *because it cost a live game*.
- A director can orphan their own game with a bad `ownerId` and be permanently locked out, since
  update, delete and list all key on it.

The test at `tests/rules/firestore.rules.test.ts:233` covers an anonymous user and a stranger taking
ownership — not the owner giving it away.

**Fix:** `&& request.resource.data.ownerId == resource.data.ownerId` on the owner branch. The check-in
branch is already safe via `hasOnly(['players'])`.

### M4 — Two default prize structures that disagree

**`useTournament.ts:118-138`** and **`BuyInSection.tsx:110-145`** / **`:155-185`** each define
fallbacks for the same fields, and they differ:

| Field | Engine default | Buy-in tab fallback |
|---|---|---|
| `allowRebuys` | `true` | `false` |
| `maxRebuys` | `3` | `0` ("unlimited") |
| `rebuyPeriodLevels` | `5` | `3` |
| `manualPayouts` | 60 / 30 / 10 | 50 / 30 / 20 |

For any field absent from the stored structure, the Payouts panel shows one split and the game pays
another.

**Fix:** one exported `DEFAULT_PRIZE_STRUCTURE`, imported by both.

### M5 — A free game reopens at £10; a dateless season invents twelve games

- **`BuyInSection.tsx:158`** — `setBuyInAmount(p.buyIn || 10)`. A free tournament (buy-in 0) reads
  back as 10 the next time the tab is opened. Free games are normal at a home league.
- **`TournamentInfoCard.tsx:102`** and **`:196`** — `displaySeason?.numberOfGames || 12`. A season
  created deliberately *without* a game count displays "Game 4 of 12". `lib/seasonProgress.ts:118,162`
  correctly uses `?? 0` and treats absent as "never advertises itself as finished", so the model and
  the card disagree about the same season.
- **`TournamentInfoCard.tsx:185`** is worse: it **persists** `numberOfGames: chosenSeason.numberOfGames || 12`
  into settings, writing the invented 12 onto a season that deliberately had none.

Same root as H1 — `||` swallowing a meaningful zero or a meaningful absence.

### M6 — The paywall exists only on the button, and its switch is a build-time trap

**`client/src/hooks/useSubscription.ts`**

`isPro` gates Go Live (`QRCodeSection.tsx:38`) and season creation (`LeagueSeasonsTab.tsx:211`). No
Firestore rule references `subscriptionStatus`, so both are reachable directly from the SDK by
flipping React state or writing to Firestore. The `users` document is correctly read-only, so nobody
can *claim* pro — they simply never need to.

Two sharper edges in the same file:

- **`useSubscription.ts:8`** — `PAYMENTS_ACTIVE = !!import.meta.env.VITE_API_BASE_URL` **fails open**:
  absent means everyone is Pro. And the variable is set neither in `Dockerfile` (which runs
  `npx vite build`) nor in `.github/workflows/deploy.yml`'s build env. Vite inlines it at **build**
  time, so setting it as a *runtime* variable on Railway will silently do nothing. Switching payments
  on is a trap laid for future-you. A payments flag should fail closed.
- **`useSubscription.ts:29`** — the `onSnapshot` error handler sets `isPro(false)`, so a transient
  Firestore blip demotes a paying customer mid-session.

**Also worth deciding:** `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` deploys to **Firebase Hosting**, while
CLAUDE.md says the app runs on **Railway**. Firebase Hosting serves only the static client, so
`/api/*` — checkout and the webhook — does not exist on that target at all. Which one is production?

### M7 — `/api/create-checkout-session` is unauthenticated and takes an arbitrary uid

**`server/routes.ts:81-88`**

```js
const { uid, email } = req.body;
if (!uid) { res.status(400).json({ error: 'uid required' }); return; }
```

No Firebase ID token is verified. Anyone can POST any `uid` and any `email`, and `customer_email` goes
straight to Stripe (`:94`). In order of realism:

- a free "make Stripe email this address about StackMate Go" primitive, with no rate limit;
- every request creates a real Checkout Session against your Stripe account;
- a completed payment upgrades whatever uid was supplied — griefing and support confusion rather than
  privilege escalation.

**Fix:** require `Authorization: Bearer <Firebase ID token>`, verify with `getAuth().verifyIdToken()`,
and take `uid`/`email` from the decoded token — never from the body. Add rate limiting.

### M8 — Webhook idempotency, ordering and lifecycle

**`server/routes.ts:167-182`**

- No `event.id` dedupe. Writes are `set(…, { merge: true })` so replays are idempotent *in value*,
  which covers most of it — but not ordering.
- Stripe does not guarantee order and retries for up to three days. A retried **`invoice.paid`
  arriving after `customer.subscription.deleted` re-grants pro permanently**, because nothing ever
  re-runs the deletion.
- **`customer.subscription.updated` is unhandled by design.** A subscription going `past_due`,
  `unpaid` or `canceled`-at-period-end keeps pro forever; only a hard `deleted` downgrades. CLAUDE.md
  already flags this as a decision owed before launch.
- `checkout.session.completed` grants pro without checking `payment_status === 'paid'`.

**Fix:** store `stripeEventCreated` alongside the status and ignore older events; map
`subscription.status` to pro/free on `customer.subscription.updated`; persist processed `event.id`s.

### M9 — Check-in read-modify-writes the whole roster

**`client/src/pages/PlayerClaimView.tsx:188-206`**

`handleClaim` reads the full players array over unauthenticated REST, maps one entry, and PATCHes the
**entire array** back — no transaction, no version check, no sub-path `updateMask`.

Two players checking in within the same second: the second write is based on a snapshot taken before
the first and silently reverts it. If the director adjusts chips or eliminates someone between a
participant's read and write, the participant's stale array overwrites it. The rule's size check
(`firestore.rules:183`) catches only add/remove — and when it does, the participant gets a permission
error for a write they were entitled to make.

**Fix:** `runTransaction`, or the separate `claims` map from H3, which removes the read-modify-write
entirely.

### M11 — Device-local storage crosses accounts — **Reported in the field, not by this audit**

**Found by using the app**, not by the sweep: a director created a second login so their league could
swap directors, signed into it on a machine already used by their personal account, and was shown the
personal account's players, blind structure and settings.

`client/src/hooks/useTournament.ts:198`, `:214`, `:220`, `:232` read `tournamentSettings`,
`tournamentBlindLevels`, `tournamentPrizeStructure` and `tournamentLocalProgress` at mount. None of
those keys carries a uid, none records who wrote it, and every one is restored unconditionally.
`client/src/hooks/useAuth.ts:117` clears exactly one key on sign-out — the live-game pin — and its
comment states the intent plainly: the rest stay "so signing back in resumes where you left off".
That assumption holds for one person and fails for two.

Also affected: `leagueSettings` / `leagueSettings:<leagueId>` (the points system),
`activeLeagueId`, `tournamentLocalGameId` and `recentPlayers` — the last of which offered one
director's real player names as autocomplete to the next.

**Not a cross-account data leak in Firestore.** Nothing stored server-side crossed over, and the
second account could not read or damage the first one's saved games: `activeDirectorTournamentId` is
cleared on logout *and* ownership-checked at `TournamentDirector.tsx:72`. The exposure was confined to
one browser, to whoever already had physical access to it.

**This audit missed it** because the sweep looked at what each storage key held and who could read it
from outside, and never asked what happens when the signed-in user changes underneath it. Worth
remembering as a class: state keyed by *device* in an app whose identity is keyed by *account*.

**Fixed** — `lib/scopedStorage.ts` buckets the keys by uid, and `hooks/useDirectorSetupSync.ts` moves
the setup itself into `userSettings/{uid}` so it follows the account across devices rather than
merely being quarantined per browser.

**The rollout itself had a one-time cost, which is worth recording.** Pre-bucket storage carries no
owner, so the first account to sign in after the deploy adopts it — and on the reporter's machine
that was the *new* account. It therefore inherited the personal account's setup and roster, and via
`tournamentSettings.leagueId` (which rides inside `settings` beside `isSeasonTournament` and
`seasonId`) the console came up pointing at one of the personal account's leagues. `useLeague`
resolves leagues against `ownerId == signed-in user` and was not the route in; the settings blob was,
and the post-H2 rules permit any signed-in session to `list` `seasons`/`leaguePlayers`/
`tournamentResults`, so nothing refused the standings queries. Not an ongoing hole — the claim is
stamped once per device and the cloud sync only moves settings between devices of one account — but
it is the shape of thing to expect whenever identity-scoped state is retrofitted onto storage that
never had an owner. Cleared by the Danger Zone controls (`components/DangerZone.tsx`). See CLAUDE.md for why nothing is cleared on sign-out, why
adoption copies rather than moves, and why the push direction stays closed until the pull has settled.

### M10 — Silent failures where data is lost

Of 151 `catch` sites across `client/src` and `server/`, roughly 14 rethrow, 30 report to the user, 75
log to console only, and 32 swallow entirely. Most of the swallows are UI preferences and genuinely
fine. These are not:

- **`client/src/lib/localProgress.ts:46`** — `catch {}` on the mirror that *is* the tournament when
